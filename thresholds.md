# Safe Ranges (Research Notes)

Prototype using simulated data. Not medical advice. These ranges are starting points, and each astronaut's own baseline matters too.

| Measurement | Green (normal) | Yellow (watch) | Red (act) | Source type | Space note | NASA hazard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resting heart rate | 60 to 100 bpm | 50 to 59 or 101 to 120 bpm | below 50 or above 120 bpm | Green: AHA. Yellow/red: team-set | Without gravity, your body fluids move toward your head, and your heart has to adjust. A fit astronaut may run below 60, so we compare you to your own normal. | Gravity |
| Blood pressure (mmHg) | under 120 and under 80 | 120 to 139 or 80 to 89; also low: under 90/60 | 140 or higher / 90 or higher; also 180/120 or higher = contact flight surgeon now | AHA categories. Low-BP cutoffs: team-set | Gravity usually helps pull blood toward your feet. In space that changes, so your blood pressure can behave differently than it did on Earth. | Gravity |
| Blood sugar, fasting (mg/dL) | 70 to 99 | 100 to 125 or 54 to 69 | 126 or higher, or under 54 | American Diabetes Association categories | Stress, a changed routine, and different food and exercise can all nudge your blood sugar up or down. | Isolation / closed environment |
| Body temperature | 97 to 99°F (36.1 to 37.2°C) | 99.1 to 100.3°F or 95 to 96.9°F | 100.4°F or higher, or under 95°F | CDC (fever at 100.4°F, hypothermia under 95°F). Yellow bands: team-set | Warm air doesn't rise in space, so heat can linger around your body. And in a small shared cabin, a fever matters because illness can spread. | Closed environment |
| Sleep (hours) | 7 or more | 6 to 6.9 | under 6; also 3 nights in a row under 6 | Green: CDC. Yellow/red: team-set | The station circles Earth about 16 times a day, so the sun seems to rise and set constantly. That makes good sleep harder to get. | Closed environment / isolation |
| Mood (1 to 5) | 3 to 5 | 2 | 1; also 3 days in a row at 2 or lower | Team-set (our own scale) | Months in a small space, far from family and friends, can wear on anyone. A low mood is a signal, not a weakness. | Isolation |
| Daily radiation dose | 0.5 mSv/day or less | above 0.5 up to 1.0 mSv/day | above 1.0 mSv/day, or any solar particle event alert | Reference range: NASA. Cutoffs: team-set | Space has far less protection from radiation than Earth does. Your total over a whole career is capped at 600 mSv, so we keep a running count. | Radiation |
